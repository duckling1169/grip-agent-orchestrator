---
name: orchestrate-brainstorm
description: Run independent ideation, blind judging and practical validation before choosing a direction.
---

Capture the goal, hard constraints, preferences, budget and success criteria. User instructions override these defaults. Daily games have 3–10 rounds per session and take under about five minutes.

1. **Diverge.** Normally delegate to three independent ideators on different available engines; for a deep run use six. Choose models, effort and broad lenses for the task, respecting quota and user preferences. Give each the same brief, its lens and the creative-ideation skill. Give no peer ideas, banned lists, examples of solutions or leader preference. Keep explicit user prohibitions. Use separate output files and fresh sessions. If an engine or worker fails, report it and retry within budget; use independent sessions if fewer engines are available.
2. **Converge.** After all ideators finish, anonymize and cluster ideas by mechanism and user value. Record cluster membership and provenance separately. Select distinct finalists and give them, the brief and the criteria to one fresh blind judge. Withhold origins, prior scores and your preference. Randomize order and reverse close comparisons. Judge each criterion with reasons and uncertainty; do not use weighted totals. Search prior art for finalists and record links, meaningful differences and uncertainty.
3. **Validate.** Give one independent paper playtester each finalist's rules and five varied concrete instances. Show available information, a simple repeatable strategy and an informed alternative, their decisions and outcomes. Name the dominant strategy, when it fails and what knowledge changes a decision. If outcomes cannot be computed, mark the result inconclusive and name the cheapest check; never invent scores. For non-games use five realistic cases and test the simplest repeated workaround.
4. **Apply four gates.** Assess (a) depth after repetition and whether the shortcut dominates; (b) usable mouse and touch input, feedback and correction without motor skill driving the score; (c) rounds and total session time; and (d) a concrete art direction. For non-games translate these into repeated-use value, access modes, workflow length and visual identity. Paper testing screens ideas; real users and devices must verify interaction. For any UI build, use frontend-design and impeccable to write an art brief with subject-specific palette, typography, layout, imagery, motion and references or examples before implementation.
5. **Decide.** Report distinct finalists, evidence, failed or inconclusive gates, prior art, cost and the next test. Revise and retest once if justified within budget; allow no winner. Bring finalists to the user for a decision unless they explicitly said to pick and build. Store progress and worker IDs in task notes and resume on worker completion; do not poll or wait inside a leader turn.
